Each type encodes distinct visual features in parallel for transmission to the brain. How many such â€˜output channelsâ€™ exist and what each encodes are areas of intense debate. In the mouse, anatomical estimates range from 15 to 20 channels, and only a handful are functionally understood. By combining two-photon calcium imaging to obtain dense retinal recordings and unsupervised clustering of the resulting sample of more than 11,000 cells, here we show that the mouse retina harbours substantially more than 30 functional output channels. These include all known and several new ganglion cell types, as verified by genetic and anatomical criteria. Therefore, information channels from the mouse eye to the mouse brain are considerably more diverse than shown thus far by anatomical studies, suggesting an encoding strategy resembling that used in state-of-the-art artificial vision systems."/><meta name="DCTERMS.hasPart" content="doi:10.5061/dryad.d9v38/1"/><meta name="DCTERMS.isReferencedBy" content="doi:10.1038/nature.16468"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="retina"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="ganglion cell"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="clustering"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="cell types"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="early vision"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="mouse"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="mammal"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="two-photon"/><meta name="DC.subject" content="machine learning"/><meta name="DC.title" content="Data from: The functional diversity of retinal ganglion cells in the mouse"/><meta name="DC.type" content="Article"/></head><!--[if lt IE 7 ]> <body class="ie6"> <![endif]-->

<div class="article-abstract">
<b>Abstract</b>
<br/>In the vertebrate visual system, all output of the retina is carried by retinal ganglion cells. Each type encodes distinct visual features in parallel for transmission to the brain. How many such â€˜output channelsâ€™ exist and what each encodes are areas of intense debate. In the mouse, anatomical estimates range from 15 to 20 channels, and only a handful are functionally understood. By combining two-photon calcium imaging to obtain dense retinal recordings and unsupervised clustering of the resulting sample of more than 11,000 cells, here we show that the mouse retina harbours substantially more than 30 functional output channels. These include all known and several new ganglion cell types, as verified by genetic and anatomical criteria. Therefore, information channels from the mouse eye to the mouse brain are considerably more diverse than shown thus far by anatomical studies, suggesting an encoding strategy resembling that used in state-of-the-art artificial vision systems.</div>
