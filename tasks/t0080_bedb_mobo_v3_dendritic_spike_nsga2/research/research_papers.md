# Research in existing papers for t0080: Bed B v3 MOBO with dendritic-spike machinery and NSGA-II

## Task Objective

Extend the t0078 AIS-augmented Bed B v2 substrate by adding dendritic-spike machinery (Mg-block NMDA
at all dendritic compartments plus Nav1.6 / NaP at distal-dendrite densities) and switching the
optimiser from BoTorch qLogNEHVI to NSGA-II via pymoo. Enforce hard biological lower bounds on AIS
Nav density (Kole 2008 prior) and the AIS-to-soma Nav ratio (Werginz 2024 measurement) so the
optimiser cannot collapse to the AIS-disabled corner observed at t0078 iter 81. The pass criterion
is a Pareto cell with DSI >= 0.4 AND PD rate >= 10 Hz, anchored to RivlinEtzion 2012 stable-cell
joint distribution and Trenholm 2013 peak-rate / Gaussian-convolution conventions. This paper review
extracts the prior densities, kinetic schemes, and threshold criteria that must constrain the v3
substrate and answer asset.

## Category Selection Rationale

Eight project categories were consulted. `direction-selectivity` and `retinal-ganglion-cell` provide
the experimental DSI / firing-rate targets and the ON-OFF DSGC type identity.
`dendritic-computation` covers the active-dendrite mechanisms being added (dendritic Na spikes, NMDA
Mg-block nonlinearity, Ca plateaus). `voltage-gated-channels` supplies the AIS Nav prior (Kole
2008), the Nav1.6 / NaP kinetic schemes (Khaliq 2003), and the Hay 2011 SK_E2 / CaDynamics_E2
sources for the slow-AHP. `compartmental-modeling` covers DSGC-specific NEURON implementations
(Schachter 2010, Poleg-Polsky 2016 Neuron, deRosenroll 2026, Poleg-Polsky 2026). `patch-clamp`
papers (Sivyer 2013, Oesch 2005, Werginz 2024, Werginz 2020) supply the dendritic- spike thresholds
and the per-compartment density measurements. `synaptic-integration` and `cable-theory` provide the
impedance-gradient + NMDA framework (Branco 2010) that motivates the distal Nav placement.

The fly-vision morphology categories were excluded as off-target. Pure starburst-amacrine-cell
modelling papers were not pulled in at this stage because the SAC release machinery is held fixed at
the t0078 substrate setting.

## Key Findings

### Dendritic Na spikes are necessary to reach DSI >= 0.7 in DSGCs

Sivyer and Williams [Sivyer2013] performed dual soma + terminal-dendrite whole-cell recordings in
rabbit ON-OFF DSGCs and showed that preferred-direction moving bars elicit fast dendritic spikes
that **precede the somatic AP by several milliseconds** and are **larger at the dendritic site than
at the soma** — the canonical Larkum-style amplitude-and-latency signature of local dendritic
spike initiation. Bath TTX at low dose selectively attenuates the dendritic-spike component while
leaving subthreshold EPSPs largely intact, **collapsing the somatic spike DSI toward zero**,
demonstrating that the high somatic DSI is generated by dendritic spike initiation rather than by
somatic threshold integration. Their compartmental model only reproduces these behaviours when
terminal dendrites are endowed with **physiologically plausible densities of voltage-gated sodium
and calcium channels**; passive-only (gNa = gCa = 0) models fail to reproduce spike-like events or
the observed preferred / null directional gain.

Oesch et al. [Oesch2005] supply the quantitative DSI numbers in rabbit ON-OFF DSGCs: spike-based DSI
**0.67 +/- 0.13 (ON) and 0.74 +/- 0.13 (OFF)**, while PSP-based (spike-blanked) DSI is only **0.09
+/- 0.05 (ON) and 0.14 +/- 0.08 (OFF)** — spike generation sharpens tuning by roughly **6-fold**.
Focal somatic TTX unmasks a bimodal spike-amplitude distribution: small dendritic spikelets at **7.4
+/- 1.9 mV** and somatic spikes at **54.8 +/- 3.5 mV**. Dendritic TTX application reduces
light-evoked spikes by **41.5 +/- 15%** but reduces depolarisation-evoked spikes by only **25.2 +/-
7.8%**, a direct demonstration that light-evoked spikes originate in the dendrites. This sets a hard
target for the v3 substrate: t0078's passive-dendrite Pareto front pinned at PD ~ 2.86 Hz / DSI 0.32
cannot escape that corner without distributed dendritic Nav.

The Schachter et al. compartmental DSGC model [Schachter2010] reaches **spike DSI ~0.8** from **PSP
DSI ~0.2** (a ~4x amplification) using **40 mS/cm^2 uniform dendritic Nav** (with an alternative 45
mS/cm^2 proximal -> 20 mS/cm^2 distal gradient tested for robustness) and **150 mS/cm^2 somatic
Nav**. Local dendritic input resistance scales from **150-200 MOhm** proximally to **>1 GOhm** at
distal tips, so a single excitatory synapse of **~1 nS** can trigger a local spike at the tip but
**3-4 nS** is required near the soma. This places the v3 distal Nav density bound at the same order:
~10-50 mS/cm^2 is the band where dendritic spikes initiate but do not run away.

### NMDA Mg block multiplicatively scales the EI-tuned signal in mouse DSGCs

Poleg-Polsky and Diamond [PolegPolsky2016a] report that NMDAR blockade with AP5 in mouse DRD4-GFP
DSGCs reduces preferred-direction PSPs by **35%** and null-direction PSPs by **34%** (n = 19) —
proportional, multiplicative reduction; **DSI is unchanged** (p > 0.5). The PD NMDAR-mediated PSP
component is **5.8 +/- 3.1 mV** vs ND **3.3 +/- 2.8 mV** (p = 0.001). The multiplicativity is
voltage-dependent: removing extracellular Mg2+ collapses the slope angle from **62.5 +/- 14.2
degrees** (multiplicative) to **45.5 +/- 5.3 degrees** (additive, p < 0.001) and significantly
reduces DSI. Their NEURON model uses **177 AMPAR + 177 NMDAR + 177 GABA_A synapses** distributed
homogeneously on ON dendrites with Jahr-Stevens-type Mg2+ block kinetics, on a morphologically
reconstructed mouse DRD4 DSGC (ModelDB 189347). The model reproduces the multiplicative scaling
**without dendritic spikes**, indicating that passive dendrites + NMDA Mg block can produce the
multiplicative gating but require tuned GABAergic inhibition for it to reach DSI >= 0.4 robustly.

Branco et al. [Branco2010] establish the underlying biophysical principle in cortical pyramidal
dendrites: **dendritic impedance gradients combined with NMDA non-linearity generate
direction-selective summation**. IN-direction (tip-to-soma) sequences produce somatic EPSPs **31 +/-
4% larger** than OUT-direction sequences (p < 0.0001, n = 20), with **38 +/- 9% higher spike
probability** (p = 0.0013). D-AP5 abolishes the supralinearity (**223 +/- 9%** of linear sum ->
**103 +/- 3%**, p < 0.0001) and reduces the IN-vs-OUT difference to a non-significant 0.4 +/- 0.4
mV. Optimal velocity for direction sensitivity is **2.0-2.6 um/ms** (matched to the DSGC bipolar
wave-speed range). The mechanism is generic: it requires only an impedance gradient and NMDA
receptors, no active dendritic Nav. This is the v3 substrate's null hypothesis: NMDA Mg block alone
may suffice to lift the Pareto front; if it does, the Nav1.6 / NaP additions are diagnostic rather
than essential.

### Mouse DSGC peak firing rates are 198 Hz preferred (control) under Trenholm peak-rate convention

Trenholm et al. [Trenholm2013] is the canonical source for mouse Hb9::eGFP DSGC peak firing rates
under the Gaussian-convolved-spike-train convention used by t0080's pass criterion. Under control
conditions, peak preferred-direction rate is **198 +/- 14 Hz** and peak null-direction rate is **27
+/- 12 Hz**, giving a **peak-rate DSI of (198 - 27) / (198 + 27) = 0.76**. Under picrotoxin (GABA-A
block), peak preferred rises to **244 +/- 18 Hz** and peak null to **202 +/- 14 Hz**. The pass
criterion's "PD rate >= 10 Hz" is interpreted as a peak rate from spike trains convolved with sigma
= 25 ms Gaussian kernels (the protocol is fully specified in the paper: 300 x 300 um bar, 96% Weber
contrast, 600 um/s, eight directions). Excitatory current at half-maximum spike rate is **88 +/- 22
pA** rising vs **193 +/- 36 pA** falling (p = 0.029, n = 7), revealing an intrinsic gain-control
conductance with **recovery tau = 604 +/- 158 ms** — a slow process that t0078 already targets via
the `tau_ca_multiplier` knob and the SK_E2 channel.

### AIS Nav density priors: Kole 2008, Werginz 2020, Werginz 2024

Werginz et al. [Werginz2024] provides the most directly applicable prior for the t0080 AIS
hard-bound check: a NEURON multi-compartment model of mouse alpha-RGCs with a five-tier partition
(dendrites, soma, soma-AIS, AIS, axon). Table 1 of that paper reports **gNa = 1300 mS/cm^2 at the
AIS** vs **75 mS/cm^2 at the soma**, an **AIS-to-soma Nav ratio of 17.3x**, and **gK = 800 mS/cm^2
AIS vs 48 mS/cm^2 soma** (ratio 16.7x). These are the values that t0078's iter 81 cell **violated by
five orders of magnitude** (its `nav16_ais` collapsed to 1e-5 S/cm^2 with an AIS-to-soma ratio of
5.5e-5 vs Werginz's measured 17.3). The published mouse alpha-RGC Nav range at the AIS, normalised
to S/cm^2, is approximately 0.7-2.0 S/cm^2 across the three alpha RGC types reported.

Kole et al. [Kole2008] is the landmark cortical-pyramidal AIS Nav patch-clamp study that established
the **0.25-0.5 S/cm^2** AIS Nav range as the canonical biophysical-modelling prior. The exact
patch-clamp values were not extracted from the paper PDF (the file was paywalled in the project's
previous download attempts and the local summary is built from CrossRef metadata only — not found
in paper). The 0.25-0.5 S/cm^2 range is cited consistently in downstream biophysical-modelling work
[Hay2011, Werginz2020, Werginz2024] as a robust lower bound. The t0080 plan adopts the Kole **lower
bound 0.25 S/cm^2** as the hard floor for `nav16_ais`.

Werginz et al. [Werginz2020] confirms in mouse OFF-alpha transient RGCs that AIS Nav1.6 density and
AIS length vary systematically across the retina, with the **AIS-to-soma Nav density ratio
approximately 7x** (lower than the alpha-ON sustained 17.3x of Werginz 2024 but consistent with the
same mouse RGC family). The plan's hard floor of **AIS-to-soma Nav ratio
> = 5** sits below the lowest measured RGC value, so any biologically plausible solution remains
> inside the search space.

### Nav1.6 + NaP kinetics: Khaliq 2003 + Hay 2011 sources for the v3 dendritic insertion

Khaliq et al. [Khaliq2003] is the source paper for the Raman-Bean-style state-machine Nav1.6 model
with explicit open-channel block, the formalism used by every downstream NaR mod-file derivative.
The published kinetic scheme (closed-inactivated-open-blocked with rate constants alpha, beta,
gamma, delta, epsilon, zeta, Con, Coff, Oon, Ooff and amplitude factors a, b) is the canonical
implementation of resurgent Na current and is the upstream source of the Nav1.6 SUFFIX vendored in
the t0078 substrate. ModelDB accession **48332** distributes the canonical mod files
(`Naresurgent.mod`, `bkpkj.mod`).

Hay et al. [Hay2011] introduced the SK_E2 / CaDynamics_E2 mechanism set that t0078 uses for the
slow-AHP. Hay 2011's NEURON-based multi-objective optimisation (NSGA-style) over 22 free parameters
with 20 firing-feature objectives — population 1000 over 500 generations — produced **~2000
acceptable L5b PC models** in ModelDB **139653** that simultaneously match perisomatic step-current
f-I targets and BAC firing. **This paper is the methodological precedent for using NSGA-II on a
multi-compartment biophysical model**: their multi-objective + evolutionary algorithm proved that
NSGA-style optimisation can recover the joint Pareto-acceptable set (perisomatic + BAC) where
single-objective fits cannot. Hay 2011's apical Nat density of **101-133.5 pS/um^2**
(BAC-acceptable) and apical Kv3.1 of **0-3.05 pS/um^2** are the biological-prior bounds on the
dendritic active conductances. NaP is included in their parameter set with **somatic gNap upper
bound 100 pS/um^2** — the v3 distal NaP upper bound should sit at or below this value.

### Bed B substrate ancestry and the dendritic-spike question

Poleg-Polsky and Diamond's mouse DRD4 DSGC model [PolegPolsky2016a] is the immediate ancestor of Bed
B's passive-dendrite design. Their NEURON model reproduces multiplicative NMDA scaling **without
dendritic spikes**, indicating that passive dendrites are sufficient to recover the PD:ND ratio —
but only on the joint manifold spanned by tuned GABA inhibition and NMDA Mg block. deRosenroll et
al. [deRosenroll2026] is the substrate that t0024 ported and t0080 extends. Their correlated-release
model reaches **DSI ~ 0.39** under faithful E/I co-correlation but **DSI ~ 0.25** under uncorrelated
release; this confirms that **the passive Bed B dendrites cannot reach DSI 0.4 robustly without
additional active dendritic mechanisms**, which is exactly the t0078 finding the v3 substrate is
designed to fix.

Poleg-Polsky [PolegPolsky2026] enumerates the broader space of DSGC dendritic mechanisms via machine
learning over a 352-segment compartmental model. The author identifies multiple biophysical
configurations that can produce robust DSI: (i) velocity-dependent coincidence detection on
dendrites, (ii) distance-graded passive-dendrite delay lines, (iii) NMDA-mediated multiplicative
gating, and (iv) hybrid combinations. **These primitives complement the SAC-driven inhibitory
mechanism rather than replacing it.** The t0080 v3 substrate is exploring the (i) + (iii) corner of
this space — coincidence detection via dendritic Nav1.6 / NaP + NMDA Mg-block — while keeping
the SAC inhibition machinery intact.

### Dendritic Ca plateaus as an alternative active mechanism

Larkum, Zhu, and Sakmann [Larkum1999] established the BAC firing mechanism in L5 pyramidal cells:
the apical dendrite contains a discrete **Ca2+ spike initiation zone** near the main bifurcation.
Distal depolarisation alone requires a high threshold (~ -50 mV); paired with a backpropagating
somatic AP within a **5-10 ms coincidence window**, the threshold drops to a distal depolarisation
of only **5-10 mV** above rest. The Ca2+ plateau lasts **30-50 ms**, far longer than an EPSP, and
produces a stereotyped **3-4 spike burst** at the soma at **100-200 Hz** instantaneous frequency.
This is the t0080 fallback mechanism if the Nav1.6 / NaP v3 substrate does not lift the Pareto front
— the project would pivot to inserting a Ca-plateau zone (Ca_LVA + SK_E2 with reduced Ca decay)
per the Hay 2011 hot-zone formalism.

### Local subunit independence and the inhibitory-veto interpretation

Schachter et al. [Schachter2010] quantify the conductance asymmetry between dendritic-spike
**initiation** vs **propagation**: initiation can be vetoed by **a few nS of inhibition** (the
measured ~ 4-10 nS DSGC null-direction inhibitory conductance), but propagation of an already-
initiated spike requires **~85 nS** — an order of magnitude larger than the measured value. So
inhibition acts locally as a spike-initiation veto, not as a global shunt. **Each dendritic spike
that initiates propagates 1:1 to the soma.** This dictates the v3 substrate's distal Nav density
window: high enough that synaptic drive of a few nS can initiate a spike at the tip (local input
resistance > 1 GOhm), low enough that the local null inhibition can still veto it.

## Methodology Insights

* **NSGA-II precedent is Hay 2011's multi-objective evolutionary algorithm.** Hay et al. [Hay2011]
  used population 1000 over 500 generations with 20 features and 22 parameters; t0080's pop 96 over
  40 generations is more aggressive (3,840 evaluations vs Hay's ~500,000) but targets a smaller
  dimensionality (~55 d vs Hay's 22 d) under a tighter cost cap. The Hay parameter analysis pattern
  — comparing union of acceptable parameter ranges vs single-target fits — should be applied at
  the t0080 results-analysis stage to identify which of the ~5-7 dendritic-spike parameters are
  necessary vs sufficient.

* **Distal dendritic Nav range: 10-50 mS/cm^2 (0.01-0.05 S/cm^2).** Schachter et al. [Schachter2010]
  used uniform 40 mS/cm^2 with a tested gradient of 45 -> 20 mS/cm^2 proximal to distal. Sivyer and
  Williams [Sivyer2013] argue qualitatively for "physiologically plausible densities comparable to
  mammalian central neurons" without giving a numerical value (not found in paper for an exact gNa
  value at terminal dendrites). The v3 plan should bound `nav16_dend_distal` at **[0, 0.05] S/cm^2**
  with a Latin-Hypercube initial sample biased toward 0.02-0.04 S/cm^2.

* **Distal NaP upper bound: 100 pS/um^2 = 0.01 S/cm^2.** Hay et al. [Hay2011] use somatic gNap =
  0-100 pS/um^2 = 0-0.01 S/cm^2 (Table 2). The v3 NaP at distal dendrites should not exceed this
  somatic upper bound — placing the NaP at higher density than the soma is not biologically
  supported and would risk runaway depolarisation, exactly the t0080 risk flagged in the task
  description.

* **NMDA Mg-block kinetics: Jahr-Stevens.** Poleg-Polsky and Diamond [PolegPolsky2016a] use
  Jahr-Stevens-type Mg2+ block in their published mouse DRD4 DSGC NEURON model (ModelDB 189347). The
  v3 substrate should use the same `Exp2NMDA` mechanism with Jahr-Stevens Mg2+ block parameters as
  the t0078 substrate (already vendored from t0008 via ModelDB 189347), with voltage offsets and
  Mg2+ concentration as MOBO free parameters (3 new parameters: `gnmda_dend`, `mg_conc`,
  `voff_nmda`).

* **AIS Nav hard floor: 0.25 S/cm^2 (Kole 2008 lower bound) — but expect optimal range 0.7-2.0
  S/cm^2.** Per [Kole2008] the published cortical-pyramidal AIS Nav range is **0.25-0.5 S/cm^2**
  (Kole2008 PDF was paywalled at download time; the range is cross-referenced from downstream papers
  including [Hay2011] and [Werginz2024], not extracted from the source paper directly — verify
  against the manual PDF before publication). Werginz et al. [Werginz2024] place mouse alpha-RGC AIS
  Nav at **1.3 S/cm^2** (calculated from 1300 mS/cm^2). Kole's lower bound is the conservative hard
  floor; the LHS initial population should bias toward 0.7-2.0 S/cm^2.

* **AIS-to-soma Nav ratio hard floor: 5x — measured RGC value is 7-17.3x.** [Werginz2024] measures
  **17.3x for alpha-ON sustained mouse RGCs**; [Werginz2020] measures **~7x for OFF-alpha transient
  mouse RGCs**. The v3 plan's hard floor of **5** sits below the lowest measured RGC value but above
  the t0078 iter 81 value of 5.5e-5 by five orders of magnitude. Bed B is a DRD4 DSGC, not an
  alpha-RGC, so the exact target ratio is unknown — but no DSGC patch-clamp study has reported
  AIS-to-soma Nav ratios below the alpha range, so 5 is a defensible biological lower bound.

* **Reference firing-rate target: peak-rate (Gaussian-convolved) NOT mean-rate.** Trenholm et al.
  [Trenholm2013] report **peak preferred-direction rate of 198 +/- 14 Hz** under the sigma = 25 ms
  Gaussian-convolution protocol. The t0080 pass criterion of **PD rate
  > = 10 Hz** must be computed under the same convention to be directly comparable.

* **Slow-AHP recovery tau ~ 600 ms.** Trenholm et al. [Trenholm2013] measure intrinsic gain-control
  recovery **tau = 604 +/- 158 ms**, set by Na slow inactivation or Ca-activated K. The t0078
  substrate already implements this via SK_E2 / CaDynamics_E2 with `tau_ca_multiplier` MOBO knob
  upper bound 20x; the v3 plan keeps this bound unchanged.

* **Local subunit-independence test: dendritic Nav density bounded above by what passes the
  `dendritic spike doesn't recruit sibling branches at rest` test from Sivyer and Williams
  [Sivyer2013].** A practical regression-style check: at the **substrate regression cell** (t0076
  iter 424 mapped to v3), dendritic Vm in one branch should not drive somatic AP without coincident
  excitation in at least one other branch. If the v3 substrate violates this, the Nav1.6 / NaP upper
  bounds should be tightened before launching NSGA-II (already in the plan's risks section).

* **Hypothesis to test (positive case): NMDA Mg-block alone is sufficient to lift the Pareto front
  to DSI 0.4 / PD 10 Hz, without distal Nav1.6 / NaP.** Per [PolegPolsky2016a] and [Branco2010],
  passive dendrites + NMDA Mg block + tuned inhibition can produce multiplicative EI gating that
  preserves DSI. If the NSGA-II solution sits at high `gnmda_dend` and low `nav16_dend_distal`, this
  hypothesis is corroborated.

* **Hypothesis to test (negative case): the t0078 +36% HV improvement was substrate-bound; v3
  substrate produces no further HV gain.** If the v3 final HV does not exceed t0078's 11.41, this is
  a strong negative result documenting that the de Rosenroll substrate's morphology / SAC release
  machinery is the bottleneck, not the dendritic biophysics. The project would then pivot to
  dendritic Ca plateaus per [Larkum1999] or substrate redesign.

* **Best practice: report Pareto-acceptable parameter ranges, not just the best single cell.** Per
  the [Hay2011] ensemble-as-experiment pattern, the t0080 results should report the union of NSGA-II
  Pareto-non-dominated parameter ranges for the new dendritic-spike parameters alongside the single
  closest-to-joint cell, to identify which knobs are necessary vs sufficient.

## Gaps and Limitations

* **Sivyer 2013 PDF is paywalled in the project.** Specific quantitative values for terminal
  dendritic gNa and gCa densities in Sivyer and Williams's compartmental model are flagged in the
  project summary as "to verify once the paper is manually downloaded — not found in paper". The
  v3 distal-Nav density range is therefore set by [Schachter2010] (40 mS/cm^2 uniform; 45 -> 20
  proximal-distal gradient) rather than by the Sivyer-Williams compartmental model directly.

* **Kole 2008 PDF was paywalled at the project's previous download attempts.** The 0.25-0.5 S/cm^2
  AIS Nav range is cross-referenced from downstream biophysical-modelling papers ([Hay2011],
  [Werginz2024]) rather than extracted from the source paper directly. Verify the Kole AIS Nav range
  against the manual PDF before publishing the v3 answer asset.

* **No DSGC patch-clamp study reports AIS-to-soma Nav ratio directly.** The 5x hard floor is
  inferred from non-DSGC RGCs ([Werginz2020] OFF-alpha transient ~ 7x; [Werginz2024] alpha-ON
  sustained ~ 17.3x). Whether DRD4 ON-OFF DSGCs sit in the same range is unknown. This is a known
  limitation of the project's biological-prior checklist and should be flagged in the answer asset.

* **No published DSGC compartmental model has joint Mg-NMDA + dendritic Nav1.6 + NaP at
  distal-dendrite densities.** Schachter et al. [Schachter2010] use Nav1.6 + Kv on dendrites but not
  NMDA Mg-block; Poleg-Polsky and Diamond [PolegPolsky2016a] use NMDA Mg-block but passive
  dendrites; Poleg-Polsky [PolegPolsky2026] enumerates them but does not publish the joint
  configuration as a deposited model. The t0080 v3 substrate is the first published
  joint-configuration model in the project's lineage.

* **Trenholm 2013's 198 Hz preferred peak rate is for the Hb9::eGFP superior-coding DSGC
  population.** The Bed B substrate is a DRD4 DSGC (after [PolegPolsky2016a] and [deRosenroll2026]),
  which is a different molecular subtype. Whether DRD4 ON-OFF DSGCs reach the same 198 Hz peak in a
  comparable in vitro stimulus protocol is not directly confirmed by the literature reviewed —
  flagged for the t0080 results discussion.

* **The Bed B Poleg-Polsky 2016 NEURON model (ModelDB 189347) has 177 NMDARs distributed
  homogeneously across ON dendrites only.** Adding NMDARs to OFF dendrites (the t0080 plan's "all
  dendritic compartments" choice) goes beyond the published model. This is a deliberate modelling
  choice, not a literature-validated configuration; the t0080 substrate-regression check at the
  t0076 iter-424 vector becomes the proxy validation for this extension.

## Recommendations for This Task

1. **Set the distal dendritic Nav1.6 bound at [0, 0.05] S/cm^2 with LHS bias toward 0.02-0.04
   S/cm^2** [Schachter2010, Sivyer2013]. Above 0.05 S/cm^2 risks runaway depolarisation and violates
   the local subunit-independence test of [Sivyer2013].

2. **Set the distal NaP bound at [0, 0.01] S/cm^2** [Hay2011]. Hay 2011's upper bound of 100 pS/um^2
   = 0.01 S/cm^2 for somatic gNap is the most directly comparable biophysical-prior anchor; the v3
   distal NaP should not exceed the somatic upper bound.

3. **Use Jahr-Stevens Mg2+ block kinetics on Exp2NMDA at all dendritic compartments**
   [PolegPolsky2016a]. Bound `gnmda_dend` at the same order as the existing AMPA-bipolar conductance
   in the t0078 substrate and `mg_conc` at [0.5, 2.0] mM (around the published 1.0-1.5 mM
   physiological range).

4. **Hard-bound `nav16_ais` at [0.25, 5.0] S/cm^2** [Kole2008, Werginz2024, Werginz2020]. Kole's
   0.25 S/cm^2 lower bound (from the cited 0.25-0.5 S/cm^2 AIS prior, verify-against- PDF flagged)
   is conservative; Werginz 2024's 1.3 S/cm^2 sits comfortably inside; the upper bound 5.0 S/cm^2
   covers the alpha-RGC range with headroom.

5. **Hard-bound AIS-to-soma Nav ratio at [5, 25]** [Werginz2024, Werginz2020]. 5x is below any
   measured mouse RGC value, ensuring biological plausibility; 25x is above [Werginz2024]'s 17.3x to
   allow for DRD4-DSGC-specific morphology.

6. **Use NSGA-II via pymoo with pop 96, 40 generations, SBX eta=15, polynomial mutation eta=20**
   [Hay2011 methodological precedent]. Initial population via Latin Hypercube Sampling for spread;
   reference point [0, 0] for hypervolume (matches t0076 / t0078).

7. **Pre-launch substrate regression check at t0076 iter-424 vector mapped to v3 49-d
   parameterisation.** Verify DSI within +/- 0.05 of t0076's 0.42 at PD ~ 8.34 Hz. Document the
   substrate-regression delta in `results/results_summary.md`.

8. **Compute peak firing rate from Gaussian-convolved spike trains with sigma = 25 ms**
   [Trenholm2013]. Match the publication convention so the **PD rate >= 10 Hz** pass criterion is
   interpretable against the literature.

9. **Report the union of NSGA-II Pareto-non-dominated parameter ranges for the new dendritic-spike
   parameters** [Hay2011 ensemble-as-experiment]. Identify which dendritic- spike knobs are
   necessary (range collapses to a narrow band on the Pareto front) vs sufficient (range stays
   broad).

10. **Author the answer asset around the iter-81 AIS-disabled-corner failure mode and the
    biological-prior checklist** drawn from this paper review: [Kole2008] (AIS Nav lower bound),
