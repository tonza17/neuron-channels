# Electrical Match between Initial Segment and Somatodendritic Compartment for Action Potential Backpropagation in Retinal Ganglion Cells

## Metadata

* **File**: `files/goethals_2020_axial-current-ais-nav-rgc.pdf`
* **Published**: 2020 (bioRxiv v2, 2020-12-10)
* **Authors**: Sarah Goethals, Martijn C. Sierksma, Xavier Nicol, Annabelle Reaux-Le Goazigo, Romain
  Brette
* **Venue**: bioRxiv (preprint, not peer-reviewed)
* **DOI**: `10.1101/2020.09.15.297937`
## Abstract

The action potential of most vertebrate neurons initiates in the axon initial segment (AIS), and is
then transmitted to the soma where it is regenerated by somatodendritic sodium channels. For
successful transmission, the AIS must produce a strong axial current, so as to depolarize the soma
to the threshold for somatic regeneration. Theoretically, this axial current depends on AIS geometry
and Na+ conductance density. We measured the axial current of mouse RGCs using whole-cell recordings
with post-hoc AIS labeling. We found that this current is large, implying high Na+ conductance
density, and carries a charge that co-varies with capacitance so as to depolarize the soma by ~30
mV. Additionally, we observed that the axial current attenuates strongly with depolarization,
consistent with sodium channel inactivation, but temporally broadens so as to preserve the
transmitted charge. Thus, the AIS appears to be organized so as to reliably backpropagate the axonal
action potential.

## Overview

This paper addresses the biophysical question of how the axon initial segment (AIS) of mouse retinal
ganglion cells (RGCs) ensures reliable transmission of the action potential (AP) to the soma. The
AIS is a narrow structure (~1 um diameter, ~30 um long) that must produce an axial current large
enough to depolarize the much larger somatodendritic compartment by about 30 mV to reach the
threshold for somatic AP regeneration. The theoretical framework of resistive coupling (Brette,
2013; Kole and Brette, 2018) predicts that this requires a high Na+ conductance density in the AIS,
but direct functional measurements in RGCs were lacking.

Goethals et al. combine whole-cell voltage-clamp recordings from RGCs in acutely isolated mouse
retina (P10-12) with post-hoc ankyrin-G immunolabeling to measure AIS geometry in the same cells.
This paired approach allows the authors to apply resistive coupling theory directly to the measured
current-geometry data to estimate AIS Na+ conductance density. The study reaches two major
conclusions: first, the AIS produces a large axial current (~6.7 nA mean) requiring a Na+
conductance density of at least ~1000 S/m2 (conservative) and most plausibly ~5000-5500 S/m2 (best
fit); second, this current is organized to reliably transmit the AP even under partial sodium
channel inactivation, through compensatory temporal broadening of the current pulse.

The paper provides the first direct experimental validation of resistive coupling theory in RGCs:
the inverse relation between axial current and AIS start distance is confirmed (p = 0.04), the
quadratic current-voltage relation near threshold matches predictions (slope beta ~4.2 +/- 1.7 mV),
and axial current adaptation co-varies with voltage threshold adaptation in a manner consistent with
AIS Na+ channel inactivation. Kv1 channel inactivation in the distal AIS (Kv1.2 expressed in RGC
AIS) likely produces the temporal broadening that compensates the 12-fold amplitude reduction.
## Architecture, Models and Methods

**Experimental preparation**: Acutely dissected mouse retinas from P10-12 Swiss and C57BL/6NRj mice.
Whole-cell patch-clamp from visually identified RGCs, retina mounted RGC-layer-up, perfused with
oxygenated Ringer solution at 32 degrees Celsius. Patch pipettes: borosilicate glass, 2.5-4 MOhm
open-tip resistance, K-gluconate internal solution (128 mM K-gluconate, 10 mM HEPES, 16 mM KCl, 1 mM
EGTA, 2 mM Mg-ATP, 0.5 mM Na2-GTP, pH 7.25, 275 mOsm) plus 1-2 mg/mL biocytin. Liquid junction
potential correction: -11 mV.

**Amplifier and data acquisition**: Multiclamp 700B; filtered at 10 kHz, digitized at 50 kHz
(DigiData 1440A). Series resistance compensated 80-95% electronically (18 us lag). Recordings
excluded if Rs > 25 MOhm (raw) or residual Rs > 5 MOhm after compensation.

**Axial current measurement (voltage clamp)**: Soma stepped from V0 = -60 mV to variable target
potentials in 0.5-1 mV increments. Above threshold a large abrupt inward current appears reflecting
the AIS action potential current flowing resistively to the clamped soma. Sodium channel origin
confirmed by 1 uM tetrodotoxin block (98 +/- 0.4% eliminated; n = 4). Peak current Ip and total
charge Q extracted after offline series-resistance correction (Traynelis, 1998).

**AIS geometry measurement**: Post-recording, retinas fixed in 4% PFA, immunolabeled with
anti-ankyrin-G (clone N106/36, 1:250) plus streptavidin-Alexa 594 and donkey anti-mouse-Alexa 488.
Confocal imaging (Olympus FV1000, 40x oil NA 1.3, 0.155 um x-y pixel, 0.5 um z-step). AIS start
position (Delta), length (L), proximal diameter, distal diameter measured. Mean: L = 31 +/- 6 um,
Delta = 8.6 +/- 3.3 um, proximal d = 0.9 +/- 0.3 um, distal d = 0.5 +/- 0.2 um (n = 14 cells with
paired geometry and electrophysiology).

**Resistive coupling theory for conductance density estimation**: Predicted axial current: Ip = (ENa
\- Vt) / (ra * (Delta + delta)), where delta = sqrt(d / (4 * Ri * g)) captures finite conductance
density g. Parameters: ENa ~ 70 mV, Ri = 100 Ohm.cm, d = 1 um. Mean squared error between predicted
and measured Ip minimized over g, yielding best-fit g ~ 5500 S/m2 (broad minimum ~3000-10000 S/m2).
Conservative lower bound by maximizing current over all AIS geometries: gmin = 1200 S/m2 (d = 1 um)
or 2467 S/m2 (d = 0.8 um), independent of AIS position.

**Adaptation protocols**: V0 stepped from -70 to -40 mV in 5 mV increments (0.5 s hold), followed by
activation voltage step. Voltage threshold, axial current amplitude, and current duration t50
measured at each V0.

**Sample sizes**: n = 10 cells (AP properties); n = 14 (AIS geometry); n = 15-17 (charge-
capacitance relation); n = 9 (threshold adaptation); n = 6-7 (threshold current adaptation). Data:
Zenodo record 4005629; code: github.com/romainbrette/AIS-geometry-and-axial-current.

## Results

* Mean axial current at spike initiation: **Ip = -6.7 +/- 1.8 nA** (n = 14 cells, SD).
* Conservative lower bound (AIS area only, ignoring cable geometry): **~814 S/m2** (d = 1 um) or
  **~1159 S/m2** (d = 0.7 um average diameter).
* Cable-theory lower bound (maximize current over all AIS geometries): **gmin = 1200 S/m2** (d = 1
  um) or **gmin = 2467 S/m2** (d = 0.8 um), independent of AIS position.
* Best-fit conductance density from resistive coupling theory: **g ~ 5000-5500 S/m2** (50-55 mS/cm2,
  d = 1 um), consistent with Guo et al. 2013 RGC model (5000 S/m2) and Lorincz and Nusser 2010
  structural estimate in CA1 (1870-3740 S/m2).
* Transmitted charge co-varies with somatic capacitance: slope = **31 mV** (Pearson r = 0.56, p =
  0.02; n = 17 cells), matching measured spike-to-regeneration gap of **33 +/- 5 mV**.
* Spike threshold: **-49 +/- 3.8 mV**; somatic regeneration threshold: **-16 +/- 4.6 mV**; gap: **33
  +/- 5 mV** (n = 10 cells).
* Threshold axial current: measured **~92 pA** (vs. theoretical prediction ~160 pA, d = 1 um, AIS
  midpoint 24 um from soma).
* Axial current attenuation with depolarization (V0: -60 to -40 mV): peak Ip factor **12.3 +/- 5.1**
  (n = 7); total charge Q factor **3.1 +/- 1.4** (broadening compensation).
* Threshold current attenuation (V0: -60 to -40 mV): factor < **2.5** (n = 6), confirming theory:
  threshold current It = k/Ra is geometry-determined, not conductance-dependent.
* Sodium channel half-inactivation voltage: **Vi ~ -55.8 +/- 3.1 mV** (n = 9); activation slope **ka
  ~ 4.1 +/- 2.2 mV**; ki/ka ~ **0.9 +/- 0.18**.
* AIS geometry: mean length **31 +/- 6 um**, start distance **8.6 +/- 3.3 um**, proximal diameter
  **0.9 +/- 0.3 um** (n = 14 cells).

## Innovations

### Direct Functional Measurement of AIS Nav Conductance Density in Mouse RGCs

Prior estimates in RGCs came from structural immunocytochemistry (Lorincz and Nusser, 2010: 187
Nav1.6 channels/um2 in CA1, implying 1870-3740 S/m2) or model optimization to AP shape (Kole et al.,
2008: 2500 S/m2; Guo et al., 2013: 5000 S/m2 in RGCs). This paper provides the first direct
functional estimate in mouse RGCs from axial current measurements, independent of kinetic model
assumptions, testing the biophysical constraint of soma-charging current rather than AP waveform
shape.

### Experimental Validation of Resistive Coupling Theory in RGCs

Resistive coupling theory had been validated primarily in cortical pyramidal cells and via
simulations. This paper directly tests predictions in mouse RGCs: inverse relation between axial
current and AIS position (p = 0.04, Pearson), quadratic IV near threshold (slope beta = 4.2 +/- 1.7
mV vs. theoretical ~4 mV), and best-fit g (5000-5500 S/m2) matching independent estimates from model
optimization.

### Discovery of Axial Current Adaptation and Compensation by Temporal Broadening

The first paper to directly measure adaptation of the axial current. A 12-fold amplitude reduction
over 20 mV depolarization is compensated by temporal broadening (t50 increases above -50 mV),
reducing effective charge attenuation to only ~3-fold. Kv1 inactivation in the distal AIS (Kv1.2
expressed in RGC AIS, Van Wart et al., 2007) is the proposed mechanism, analogous to AP broadening
in layer 5 pyramidal cells (Kole et al., 2007).

### Direct Demonstration of the Charge Matching Principle in RGCs

Hamada et al. (2016) provided only indirect evidence that AIS position covaries with somatodendritic
capacitance. This paper provides the first direct measurement: Q linearly proportional to C with
slope ~31 mV, matching the spike-to-regeneration gap in the same cells.

## Main Ideas

* AIS Na+ conductance density in mouse RGCs is at minimum **~1000-1260 S/m2 (~10-12.6 mS/cm2)**
  (conservative cable theory) and most plausibly **~5000-5500 S/m2 (~50-55 mS/cm2)** (resistive
  coupling theory best fit). Both far exceed t0080 hard floor (nav16_ais >= 0.25 S/cm2), confirming
  it is biologically conservative by ~40-200x.
* AIS diameter dominates axial current through d-squared dependence of axial resistance: a 200 nm
  error (~20% of d = 1 um) causes ~50% resistance error. Compartmental models should treat AIS
  diameter as a free bounded variable, not fix it arbitrarily.
* The charge-capacitance matching principle (slope ~31 mV) implies co-regulation of AIS structural
  parameters with soma size across RGC subtypes. t0080 MOBO bounds for AIS geometry should reflect
  this biological coupling.
* Temporal broadening of the axial current (likely via Kv1 inactivation) preserves total charge
  transmission under depolarization (3-fold vs. 12-fold peak attenuation). Models tracking only peak
  Nav conductance may underestimate spike transmission robustness.
* The P10-12 mouse RGC sample yields AIS Nav density estimates in the same order of magnitude as
  adult RGC measurements (Werginz 2020 Sci. Adv.), confirming high AIS Nav density is established
  early in postnatal development.

## Summary

Goethals et al. study the biophysical organization of the axon initial segment in mouse retinal
ganglion cells, addressing how a narrow (~1 um diameter) structure reliably transmits the action
potential to the much larger soma. The AIS must produce an axial current strong enough to depolarize
the soma by ~30 mV to reach somatic spike regeneration threshold, a demanding requirement given the
geometric impedance mismatch at the axosomatic junction. Prior estimates of AIS Nav conductance
density in RGCs came only from computational model fitting to AP shape; this paper provides the
first direct functional measurement via the axial current itself.

The approach combines whole-cell voltage-clamp measurement of the axial current with post-hoc
ankyrin-G immunolabeling to measure AIS geometry in each recorded cell (P10-12 mouse retina, n =
14-17 cells). Resistive coupling theory is applied to these paired measurements to estimate AIS Nav
conductance density. Additionally, the adaptation of the axial current with membrane potential is
characterized, revealing that temporal broadening by Kv1 channel inactivation reduces effective
charge attenuation from 12-fold (peak current) to only 3-fold (total charge) over a 20 mV
depolarization.

Key quantitative results: mean axial current **-6.7 +/- 1.8 nA**; minimum Nav conductance density
from cable theory **~1200 S/m2 (d = 1 um)** or **~2467 S/m2 (d = 0.8 um)**; best-fit from resistive
coupling theory **~5000-5500 S/m2 (50-55 mS/cm2)**; charge-capacitance slope **31 mV** matching the
spike-to-regeneration gap; **12-fold peak current** versus **3-fold charge attenuation** over 20 mV
depolarization. These converge with Guo et al. 2013 model estimates (5000 S/m2) and Werginz 2020
Sci. Adv. values (~1300 mS/cm2).

For t0080, this paper provides an independent empirical lower bound on AIS Nav density in mouse RGCs
supporting the hard biological floor nav16_ais >= 0.25 S/cm2. The conservative minimum (~10-12.6
mS/cm2) exceeds this floor by ~40-50x; the best-fit (~50-55 mS/cm2) by ~200x, confirming the floor
is conservative. The paper establishes that AIS diameter is a critical free parameter (0.7-1.2 um
proximal range from measurements) and that the charge- capacitance coupling principle should inform
how AIS geometry bounds are set relative to soma size in t0080 MOBO optimization.
