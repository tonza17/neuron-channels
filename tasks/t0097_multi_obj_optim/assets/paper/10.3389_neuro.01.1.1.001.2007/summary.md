# A Novel Multiple Objective Optimization Framework for Constraining Conductance-Based Neuron Models by Experimental Data

## Metadata

* File: `files/druckmann_2007_multi-obj-opt.pdf`
* Published: 2007
* Authors: Shaul Druckmann 🇮🇱, Yoav Banitt 🇮🇱, Albert Gidon 🇮🇱, Felix Schurmann 🇨🇭,
  Henry Markram 🇨🇭, Idan Segev 🇮🇱
* Venue: Frontiers in Neuroscience, Vol. 1, Issue 1, pp. 7-18
* DOI: `10.3389/neuro.01.1.1.001.2007`

## Abstract

We present a novel framework for automatically constraining parameters of compartmental models of
neurons, given a large set of experimentally measured responses of these neurons. In experiments,
intrinsic noise gives rise to a large variability (e.g., in firing pattern) in the voltage responses
to repetitions of the exact same input. Thus, the common approach of fitting models by attempting to
perfectly replicate, point by point, a single chosen trace out of the spectrum of variable responses
does not seem to do justice to the data. In addition, finding a single error function that
faithfully characterizes the distance between two spiking traces is not a trivial pursuit. To
address these issues, one can adopt a multiple objective optimization approach that allows the use
of several error functions jointly. When more than one error function is available, the comparison
between experimental voltage traces and model response can be performed on the basis of individual
features of interest (e.g., spike rate, spike width). Each feature can be compared between model and
experimental mean, in units of its experimental variability, thereby incorporating into the fitting
this variability. We demonstrate the success of this approach, when used in conjunction with genetic
algorithm optimization, in generating an excellent fit between model behavior and the firing pattern
of two distinct electrical classes of cortical interneurons, accommodating and fast-spiking. We
argue that the multiple, diverse models generated by this method could serve as the building blocks
for the realistic simulation of large neuronal networks.

## Overview

This paper introduces the multiple objective optimization (MOO) framework that has since become the
de facto methodology for biophysically detailed single-neuron model fitting (and the foundation
underneath BluePyOpt and the Hay/Druckmann 2011 follow-on work). The authors argue that the
prevailing single-trace, single-error fitting strategy is fundamentally mismatched to in vitro
electrophysiology because repeated identical current injections produce visibly different voltage
traces in the same cell. They propose treating fitting as a Pareto problem in which several
biophysically meaningful spike features (rate, width, AHP depth, accommodation, latency, overshoot)
are scored independently in units of the experimental standard deviation, and the search returns the
non-dominated front rather than a single "winner".

The framework is demonstrated on two electrical classes of rat somatosensory cortical
interneurons - accommodating (AC) and fast-spiking (FS) - using a 12-parameter compartmental model
with 10 voltage-gated channel types at the soma and a passive dendrite/axon. The genetic algorithm
used is a custom NSGA-II implementation in NEURON, evolving a population of 300 organisms over 1000
generations. The headline finding is that the multi-objective approach (a) recovers a population of
distinct but equally acceptable model neurons rather than a single fit, (b) cleanly separates the
two electrical classes in some channel-conductance subspaces, and (c) supports principled
interrogation of which features are mutually conflicting via the per-pair Pareto fronts. The
framework is presented as a general-purpose recipe applicable to any conductance-based cell type.

## Architecture, Models and Methods

The cell substrate was a reconstructed nest basket cell interneuron implemented in NEURON 5.9 as a
**301-compartment** model. Voltage-dependent channels were inserted only at the soma; dendrites and
axon were modelled as passive cables. Ten ion channel types were included with their kinetics taken
from the experimental literature: persistent Na (Nap), transient Na (Nat), fast-inactivating K
(Kfast), slow-inactivating K (Kslow), A-type K (IA), Kv3.1, M-type K (Im), high-voltage-activated
Ca, SK Ca-activated K, and Ih. Together with somatic and dendritic leak conductances, this gave **12
free parameters** with lower bound 0 and upper bound typically **1000 mS/cm^2** (100 for Nap, Im,
Ca, SK, Ih). Two channel kinetics were shifted in voltage by hand to bring AP features into the
fittable range: Nat by **+10 mV** and Kslow by **+20 mV** (depolarizing direction).

The optimization used a custom NSGA-II in NEURON with real-valued parameters, time-diminishing
non-uniform mutation (Michalewicz 1992), simulated binary crossover (SBX, Deb and Agarawal 1995),
and a sharing function (Goldberg and Richardson 1987) for diversity. Each run evolved **300
organisms for 1000 generations**; the authors verified insensitivity to these settings up to 8000
iterations. Six error functions were used per stimulus: spike rate, accommodation index, latency to
first spike, average AP overshoot, average AHP depth, and average AP width. Each feature error was
computed as the absolute difference between model and experimental means divided by experimental SD,
then averaged across **three current levels (150, 225, 300 pA)** of 2-second steps. Experimental
data came from in vitro rat somatosensory cortex recordings (Markram et al., 2004): **15 traces per
cell** (5 repetitions x 3 currents) for one AC exemplar (AC 5) and one FS exemplar (FS 5). The MOO
ran on a 112-processor AMD Opteron cluster or a **256-512 processor BlueGene/L**; one fit took
roughly one day.

## Results

* Final summed error converged to **5-7 SD units across 6 features** for both AC and FS exemplars,
  i.e. on average **<1 SD per feature**.
* AC exemplar best fit (1000th generation) channel densities (mS/cm^2): **Nat = 359, Nap = 0.00033,
  Kfast = 423, Kslow = 242, IA = 99, Kv3.1 = 218, Ca = 0.00667, SK = 32.8, Ih = 10, Im = 0,
  Leaksoma = 0.00737, Leakdendrite = 0.001**.
* FS exemplar best fit channel densities (mS/cm^2): **Nat = 172, Nap = 0.1, Kfast = 0.5, Kslow =
  199, IA = 51, Kv3.1 = 89, Ca = 0.00153, SK = 97, Ih = 4, Im = 64, Leaksoma = 0.06,
  Leakdendrite = 0.00163**.
* With the **2 SD acceptance threshold** the Pareto front contained **300 acceptable solutions per
  class**, defining substantially-sized "clouds" rather than point estimates in 12-d parameter
  space.
* AC and FS solution clouds are **clearly segregated** along Nat (and other channels) but
  intermixed along Im and SK, identifying which conductances actually distinguish the two
  electrical classes.
* Some Pareto pairs (e.g. AP overshoot vs accommodation) show **no effective trade-off** while
  others (accommodation index vs spike rate) show a **non-trivial trade-off front**.
* For the AC class, picking different points on the spike-rate-vs-accommodation Pareto front yields
  **14 vs 13 APs** in 2 s and visibly different accommodation profiles - reproducing the natural
  trial-to-trial variability seen experimentally.
* As a proof of principle, **adding a 7th feature counting firing pauses** allowed the same
  framework to qualitatively fit a third electrical class, the stuttering interneuron, with
  conductances Nat = 479, Kfast = 482, Kslow = 477, Kv3.1 = 514 (mS/cm^2).
* The pooled normalised feature distribution of the **300 model variants closely matches the
  pooled experimental variability across 5 cells**, with minimal bias.

## Innovations

### Multi-Objective (Pareto) Fitting for Conductance-Based Neurons

First systematic application of NSGA-II-style multi-objective optimization to single-neuron
conductance fitting. Rather than weighting and summing heterogeneous error terms, the algorithm
returns the full non-dominated Pareto front across feature errors, exposing which objectives
genuinely conflict for a given cell.

### Variability-Aware Feature Errors in SD Units

Introduced the convention of measuring each feature error in units of the experimentally observed
standard deviation across repetitions. This both incorporates intrinsic neural noise into the
fitting target and makes errors directly interpretable: "1.0" means "one experimental SD off the
mean."

### Population-of-Models Output

Instead of a single best parameter vector, the framework yields a population of acceptable solutions
(e.g. 300 models within 2 SD on every feature). This formalises the idea that biological neurons of
a given class are themselves a parameter-space cloud, and the model population can substitute for
trial-to-trial variability when used as building blocks for network simulation.

### Pareto Diagnostics for Channel-Class Identification

Projecting the acceptable-solution cloud onto individual conductance axes reveals which channels
are tightly constrained, which are unconstrained, and which separate electrical classes. This
provides a principled tool for identifying class-defining biophysical parameters - directly
relevant to comparisons across cell types.

## Datasets

* In vitro whole-cell current-clamp recordings of rat somatosensory cortical interneurons from
  Markram et al. (2004), Nature Reviews Neuroscience.
* AC class: 5 accommodating cells (AC 1-5), with AC 5 used as the fitting exemplar.
* FS class: 5 fast-spiking cells (FS 1-5), with FS 5 used as the fitting exemplar.
* Per-cell sampling: **15 voltage traces per cell** = 5 repetitions x 3 step-current levels (150,
  225, 300 pA), each trace 3 s long.
* Stuttering proof-of-principle dataset: a single stuttering interneuron from the same Markram et
  al. database, 150 pA step current.
* No public dataset release is reported with the paper; data are referenced via the prior Markram
  2004 publication.

## Main Ideas

* For our project, MOO over biophysically-meaningful spike-feature errors is the methodology of
  choice when fitting somatic Na/K conductance combinations to a target tuning curve - it avoids
  the arbitrary weighting of heterogeneous errors that single-objective optimization forces.
* Returning a Pareto front of 300 acceptable parameter sets, rather than a point estimate, gives
  an immediate readout of conductance sensitivity: tightly constrained channels are those that
  really matter for the firing pattern, broadly distributed ones are degenerate dimensions.
* Measuring each feature error in units of the experimental SD is a clean way to express
  goodness-of-fit when matching our model to patch-clamp ground-truth: a converged fit should land
  every feature within ~1 SD of experiment.
* NSGA-II in NEURON with **300 organisms x 1000 generations** is the canonical reference cost
  envelope for this class of problem - useful when budgeting compute for the t0097 catalogue's
  multi-objective task.
* The Druckmann 2007 framework underwrites all later MOBO neuron-fitting work (Druckmann 2011,
  Hay 2011, BluePyOpt). Citing this paper as the methodology root in our own write-ups is
  appropriate.

## Summary

Druckmann et al. (2007) confront a methodological problem at the core of conductance-based
neuron modelling: how to automatically fit the maximal-conductance vector of a compartmental model
to noisy in vitro recordings, given that repeated identical current injections produce visibly
different voltage traces in the same cell. They argue that the dominant single-trace,
single-error-function approach is mis-specified - it ignores intrinsic variability and forces an
arbitrary weighting of heterogeneous error terms (e.g. spike timing vs spike shape).

Their solution is a multi-objective optimization framework in which several biophysically
meaningful spike features (rate, width, AHP depth, accommodation, latency, overshoot) are each
assigned their own error function, scored in units of the feature's experimental standard
deviation, and optimized jointly with a custom NSGA-II genetic algorithm running in NEURON. The
fitting target is a compartmental model with 10 somatic ion channels and 12 free conductance
parameters. Convergence was tested on two distinct cortical-interneuron electrical classes -
accommodating and fast-spiking - using 300 organisms and 1000 generations on either a 112-CPU AMD
cluster or a 256-512-processor BlueGene/L.

The framework converges to mean per-feature error of less than 1 SD for both classes and returns
hundreds of "acceptable" parameter sets within 2 SD on every feature. These solution clouds
segregate cleanly along some channels (Nat) and overlap on others (Im, SK), revealing which
conductances actually carry class identity. The Pareto fronts between feature pairs further expose
which objectives genuinely conflict, providing diagnostic information that single-objective fits
would discard. As a proof of generalisation the same recipe with one extra feature qualitatively
captures a third (stuttering) electrical class.

For the present project this paper is the methodology root. Our t0097 catalogue is structured
around multi-objective optimisation of somatic Na/K conductance combinations (and follow-on
dendritic-conductance variants) against a target angle-to-AP-frequency tuning curve in a
direction-selective retinal ganglion cell. Druckmann 2007 supplies (i) the SD-normalised
feature-error formulation that we should mirror when scoring our model against patch-clamp
ground-truth, (ii) the NSGA-II reference cost envelope (300 organisms x 1000 generations) for
budgeting compute, and (iii) the Pareto-of-models output mode, which is more diagnostic than a
single best-fit vector for downstream sensitivity and conductance-class analysis.
